Your task
This is a group assignment.

Your group has been hired by [your client] to develop an R Shiny-based interactive visualisation interface that will be tailored to [your user group].

You will select one of the following options:

Option 1: Tourism

Client   The City of Melbourne

User group   Tourists who are planning to visit Melbourne or are currently visiting Melbourne

Option 2: Government performance

Client   One of the ten departments of the Victorian Government

User group   Public servants or members of the general public interested in the performance of any State Government-related topic (transport, planning and building, health, education, ...)

Option 3: Own choice (approval required)

Client   The City of Melbourne or a Victorian Government department

User group   If you would like to target a different user group, please discuss with the Senior Tutor, Alan Thomas, and get approval from him.

Your interface must:

Be intuitive and easy to use
Be carefully designed for the needs of the target user group
Help the users to discover interesting and practical information, identify interesting patterns in the data and/or make decisions for specific tasks (as appropriate for your chosen option)
Be designed with reference to the principles of data graphics, cartography, and human-computer interaction covered in lectures
Beyond the requirements on this page, you have a large amount of freedom to design the interface in any manner you choose. You are expected to make full use of the fact that you are working in a team of four students.

In addition to the interface itself, you will prepare a design summary, a pattern or use-case summary and a video pitch to the client.

How to get started
The process of working on Assignment 3 will include the following steps:

Review the Assignment 3 briefing in Lab 8
Selection of the user group and purpose of your interface
Data selection and formatting
Some descriptive statistics and data analyses (initially to develop your own understanding of the data)
Designing the interface with the information hierarchy in mind
Developing the interface and iteratively redesigning it as you go
A final testing phase.
The key questions to ask include (but are not limited to):

Where and when will individual users use the interactive visualisation?
What patterns can be found? How can you help the user to discover patterns?
Are there any anomalies or specifics of any particular location/type of data?
What device is the user group most likely to use (computer, phone, tablet, kiosk, etc.)? You can focus on a single device type – you do not need to design a responsive interface.

Submission
This exercise is a group-based assignment.

The assessment is worth 35% of your final subject mark.

You will submit the following three parts through Canvas:

The implementation of, and a report on, your developed interface – 25% of your final subject mark
A recorded video pitch, including a demonstration of the developed tool – 10% of your final subject mark

Part A. Implementation and Report
One group member must submit two files to this assignment by the stated deadline:

A single zipped file containing your interface, any required data, and any other files that are needed to run the interface. 
A PDF report, submitted simultaneously into Canvas (not inside your zipped file), that contains three sections as follows:
I. a design summary (maximum 2 pages) explaining:

(briefly) what problems your interface solves for your target user group
how your interface is designed so that it supports this
what features of the design you would like to receive credit for
justification of the design decisions made while developing these features, with links to concepts from lectures
e.g. why a particular colour scheme is used, or why a particular chart type is the best visualisation of the underlying data
II. a pattern and/or use case summary (maximum 1.5 pages) showcasing:

some of the interesting or useful information, insights or patterns in the data that the interface helps your target user group discover, and
a short rationale as to why your tool helped in those discoveries, and how you embedded concepts from lectures into your design to enable these use cases to occur.
Tip: you may choose to invent various personas based on the target user group and present use cases for each persona.
III. any sources or references used.
